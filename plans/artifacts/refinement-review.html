Roadgen Refinement Review

The networks build, validate and drive, but they don't yet look like cities. Lane counts follow the direction a road points, not the job it does, so roads jump from four lanes to two along a straight line. About one node in eight is a dead end. Roads are tested as zero-width lines, so their bodies can overlap. The ground mesh is too coarse to stay under curved and banked roads. This review explains the six esmini screenshots, lists what is missing and what can go, and orders the work.

Based on the code on main (merge of stage B), the plans in plans/, and measurements of the example runs in out/ with plans/scripts/refinement_probe.py. November 2026. The source file for this review is plans/2026-11-06-refinement-review.md.

The six screenshots

1, 2 · Four lanes become two, and the ground covers the road

ours
Decision
The rule dates from the Phase 3–4 prompts: avenues (2, 2) and streets (1, 1), later 4-lane and 2-lane one-ways. In lsystem it became an axis rule: major_lanes along local y and minor_lanes along local x (defaults.yaml:30-31, lsystem.py:1733-1749). The rule suits a grid, but on curved, organic or map-driven growth a road's class depends on which way each segment happens to point.
Why lanes jump
  • A street snaps onto an avenue end within 15 m (resolve, lsystem.py:974) and continues it straight.
  • If travel directions differ, the two-road node stays a junction, and surplus lanes stop with no taper (junctions.py:234-253).
  • Merged roads do taper, but the taper is capped at half the edge (profiles.py:78), so on short edges it is abrupt and often inside a curve.
  • Streets spawned from highways take their class from heading (lsystem.py:1316).
Why the ground shows
  • The 20 m ground grid is levelled only at vertices within half width + 2 m + 20 m. A triangle on the road edge can reach 20·√2 ≈ 28 m, so one corner keeps terrain height and lifts the triangle through the asphalt where the road runs in a cut.
  • Banking is extrapolated 22 m beyond the edge (ground.py:135).
  • On sag curves the flat triangle lies above the road.
Options
A, chosen: hierarchy by role. After growth, join edges into strokes: the straightest continuation through each node, under about 35° deflection. Rank strokes by length × betweenness, optionally weighted by population, and assign highway / arterial / collector / local by rank. Lanes stay constant along a stroke and change only at real junctions, with a taper on a straight section. B (class per grid line) is dropped because Manhattan grids are not the target; their lines are straight strokes anyway. C (lanes from demand) can be added to A later as a weight.
Fix in esmini: work items R1 (hierarchy) and R4 (ground). Both change only the generator and the scene file.

3 · The junction is a different grey

oursesmini draws it
Why
With HD on, every hole in a junction's lane surface larger than a set area becomes an <object type="patch"> with an asphalt outline, 0.02 m above the junction plane (builder.py:682-760). esmini draws outline objects as plain coloured geometry, not with its lane surface material, so the patch looks lighter. The sidewalk and curb lanes of the corner roads add the pale bands at the corners.
Options
  • (1) Cover the holes with extra lanes or roads. This changes the junction the drive-check and validators read.
  • (2) Draw the junction surface in our scene mesh in esmini's asphalt colour. Visual only.
  • (3) Leave it for Blender.
Chosen: (2), because it fixes the look in esmini without touching anything the simulation uses. Patches are still written when no scene file is made. Nothing else in the code reads them. Work item R5.

4 · Roads disappear under the ground at the water

mostly ourspartly esmini
Why
Under bridge spans the ground is left at terrain height (ground.py:126). A land strip between two water stretches, or a coarse shore cell, stands above the deck. esmini draws no deck, piers or tunnel portals, and that part is an esmini limitation. The stair-step shoreline comes from colouring each 20 m vertex by the nearest terrain class.
Fix in esmini: clamp the ground under bridges to the deck minus clearance, add simple deck and pier geometry to the scene, and smooth the terrain-class boundaries. Work item R4. Bridge and tunnel detail beyond that can wait for Blender.

5 · Roads that lead nowhere

ours
Why
Streets that reach water or vegetation are cut at the edge and do not continue (legal_street, lsystem.py:1485-1500). Failed continuations leave free ends too. Nothing cleans them up:
  • The connector pass is off (detour_ratio: 0).
  • Cul-de-sacs are off in grid presets.
  • repair never removes a dead end.
  • --allow-traps turns the trap report into a warning.
Fix: Parish & Müller's own local constraint. Extend a free end to a nearby road; if that is not allowed, trim a short stub back to its junction; if neither works, give it a turning circle. Work item R3.

6 · One road runs into another

ours
Why
Growth tests centre lines only: a zero-width crossing test, a 15 m snap and a 30° minimum angle (acceptable, lsystem.py:1014-1051). Nothing checks the spacing between near-parallel roads or their width. The curve fit rejects only contacts it creates itself (alignment.py:297-335). The clearance validator sees only centre lines that cross.
Fix: width-aware growth (body buffer, spacing, merging near-parallel roads, a minimum angle that depends on width) and a body-overlap validator. Work item R2.

What needs to be implemented

Order: R1 → R2 → R3 → R4 → R5 → R6 → R7, with the performance items P1–P6 alongside. Each item ends with the check that says it is done.

R1

Road hierarchy and lane continuity

  1. New pure step roadgen/generators/hierarchy.py: build strokes, choosing at each node the continuation with the smallest deflection under about 35°.
  2. Rank strokes by length × edge betweenness, using seeded, sampled betweenness for speed and optionally weighting by population. Assign highway, arterial, collector and local by share of network length. Each class's lanes, speed and one-way pattern come from parameters in defaults.yaml.
  3. Remove the axis-to-class mapping in _to_graph. The connector pass and highway spurs get their class from the stroke they join.
  4. Lane changes happen only at junctions with three or more arms. The taper uses MUTCD lengths (L = W·S²/60 below 45 mph, W·S above) and must sit on a straight section. If there is no room, the change moves to the previous junction.
  5. Re-record the lsystem identity fingerprints; update CLAUDE.md and docs/lsystem.md.

Done whenthe probe finds 0 lane jumps at two-road nodes and class changes only at junctions with 3+ arms, on every preset; and the 200-seed sweeps still have 0 build failures.

R2

Width-aware growth

  1. In acceptable(), buffer each proposal by its outer half width (side lanes included) and reject it if it touches the body of a road it does not connect to. The existing spatial hash keeps this fast.
  2. Minimum spacing between near-parallel roads: (w₁ + w₂)/2 + min_block_depth.
  3. A proposal that would run alongside an existing road at under 20° snaps onto it.
  4. Minimum angle at a node grows with width, so bodies separate inside the junction trim.
  5. Add a body-overlap check to validate/clearance.py using lane polygons, excluding roads in the same junction.

Done whenbody overlaps = 0 on all presets and across the 200-seed sweeps.

R3

Dead-end policy

  1. A pass after growth, on by default. For each free end, try in order: extend to the nearest road within about 1.5 blocks (checking maps, grade, angle and width); trim a stub shorter than one block back to its junction; give it a turning circle (the culdesac transform, opened to all classes except highways).
  2. Streets that meet water or a park end at the last junction before it, not mid-segment.
  3. Turn the connector pass on in the map presets.
  4. Report the dead-end share as a metric. Remove --allow-traps from the examples once they pass without it.

Done whendead ends are under 3 % of nodes, not counting intended cul-de-sacs and roads leaving the map, and the examples run without --allow-traps.

R4

Ground that stays under the roads (esmini scene)

  1. Quick fixes in scene/ground.py:
    • level out to margin + cell·√2;
    • clamp the banking offset at the road edge;
    • under bridges, keep land below the deck minus clearance;
    • smooth the terrain-class colours.
  2. Footprint cut: subtract the union of road and junction surfaces from the ground and triangulate the rest with the road edges as constraints. Edge vertices take the road edge height, so the ground meets the road.
  3. Simple bridge decks and piers and tunnel portals in the .osgt, also usable in Blender.

Done whena poke-through check (ground above any road-surface sample) reports 0 on city, mixed_city and hill_town, and screenshots 1, 2 and 4 look clean in esmini.

R5

Junctions the same colour as roads (esmini)

  1. Check how esmini renders outline objects and their fillType. If an asphalt fill can use the road material, use that and stop here.
  2. Otherwise draw each junction's fill area in the scene mesh in the asphalt colour measured from esmini, just under the lane surfaces. Write the patch objects only when no scene is written.
  3. Check that esmini's own model and texture resources are installed. Flat colours everywhere may only mean they are missing.

Done whenscreenshot 3 shows one asphalt colour, and the drive-check and HD validation results are unchanged.

R6

Buildings that look like a city

  1. Split each block, after the sidewalk setback, into parcels by recursive oriented-box splits: frontage about 15–40 m, depth about 20–40 m.
  2. Put one building on each parcel with front and side setbacks, without the large rounded corners. Height comes from population and district style.
  3. Keep parks, vegetation and water free of buildings.

Done whenno block is a single building unless it is small, the height distribution follows population, and the views in 1, 2 and 6 show streets lined with separate buildings.

R7

Materials, textures and Blender export

  1. Export our own road mesh per lane type with UVs = (s, t), and road marks as decal strips from the <roadMark> data. Use glTF, which Blender imports directly.
  2. Give each surface a material id: asphalt, concrete sidewalk, curb, grass, water, facade.
  3. A script fetches CC0 textures (ambientCG, Poly Haven) into a git-ignored folder and records their licences.
  4. Check whether esmini's bundled OSG reads textured .osgt. If it does, the ground and buildings get textures in esmini too.

Done whena city opens in Blender with textured roads, sidewalks, ground and buildings, aligned with the .xodr.

Q

Quality gates for naturalness

Move the probe into roadgen/metrics/network.py and the sweep: dead-end share, lane jumps, class changes, body overlaps and ground poke-through. Add test thresholds so these issues cannot quietly come back.

What can go

  • The axis rule for lanes (major_* / minor_* by direction). R1 replaces it.
  • Junction patch objects when a scene file is written. R5 replaces them.
  • Corner roads in esmini runs. They cost about 27 s of city's load. Make them optional once the scene draws sidewalks round corners, and keep them for HD output.
  • The HD lane plot on every run (4.5 s). Make it opt-in.
  • Seven re-parses of the same file during validation (P2).

Still open from earlier stages

  • Divided carriageways; trumpet interchanges; roundabout splitter islands.
  • Roundabout spur failures: mixed_city, 10 of 200 seeds with HD off.
  • Drive-check for interchanges; lsystem never builds cloverleafs; city seed 7 has no street × street junctions.
  • Comparison with real-city statistics and a training-data export (review PDF, parts D and E).
